What is Body Mass Index (BMI)?

Body Mass Index, or BMI, is a simple number calculated from your height and weight. Doctors and health organizations around the world use it as a quick first screening tool to estimate whether someone's weight falls into a healthy range for their height. While it does not measure body fat directly, BMI remains one of the most widely used starting points for understanding general health and weight trends.

How your results are calculated

Your BMI is calculated as weight in kilograms divided by your height in meters squared. Your ideal weight range is the weight your body would need to be at your height to fall within the normal BMI range of 18.5 to 24.9. Your daily calorie estimate first calculates your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) — the energy your body burns at complete rest — using the Mifflin-St Jeor formula, then multiplies it by your selected activity level to estimate your Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a healthy BMI range?

For most adults, a BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 is considered within the normal range. Below 18.5 is classed as underweight, 25 to 29.9 as overweight, and 30 or above as obese.

Is BMI accurate for everyone?

BMI is a useful screening tool but does not directly measure body fat. It can be less accurate for athletes with high muscle mass, pregnant women, children, and older adults, so it is best considered alongside other health indicators.

How is BMI calculated?

BMI is calculated by dividing your weight in kilograms by the square of your height in meters.

What are BMR and TDEE?

BMR (Basal Metabolic Rate) is the energy your body needs at complete rest. TDEE (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) adds your activity level on top of BMR to estimate the total calories you burn in a day.

Is my data stored anywhere?

No. All calculations happen in your browser. Your BMI history is saved only in your browser's local storage on your own device and is never transmitted to any server.
